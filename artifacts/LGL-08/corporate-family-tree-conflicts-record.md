## 1. Request Summary

On July 20, 2026, Douglas H. Wentz requested a full conflicts search in connection with a proposed engagement by TechBridge Solutions, Inc. ("TechBridge" or the "Prospective Client"), a Delaware corporation, to act as company counsel in a controlled auction process for the sale of all outstanding equity of TechBridge. No buyer has been selected. The bidder list is expected to be finalized in late September 2026, and this record must be re-run against the bidder list before any bidder-specific work begins (see Part 9, Item 3).

The requesting attorney identified an anticipated fee of approximately $1.4 million and an anticipated signing date in the first quarter of 2027.

Because TechBridge is sponsor-backed and because the requesting attorney disclosed at intake that TechBridge's founder holds a board seat at an existing firm client, this request was routed to the enhanced review track under Section 4.2 of the firm's New Business Intake Policy rather than the standard automated clearance track.

## 2. Prospective Client and Engagement Scope

**Prospective Client.** TechBridge Solutions, Inc., a Delaware corporation, headquartered in Bellview, Calloway. Formed as TechBridge Solutions LLC (Delaware) on March 4, 2016; converted to a Delaware corporation on August 12, 2021 in connection with the sponsor's initial investment. Approximately 340 employees. Business: workflow automation software for regulated industries, sold principally to healthcare and financial-services buyers.

**Scope of proposed engagement.** Company-side representation in a sale process, including preparation of the data room, review and negotiation of the purchase agreement and ancillary documents, regulatory analysis and filings, and closing mechanics. The scope does not include representation of any individual seller, any equityholder in its individual capacity, or any member of management with respect to compensation or rollover arrangements.

**Client identity.** Under Model Rule 1.13(a) and the corresponding rule in each jurisdiction in which the firm maintains an office, the client is the corporation. It is not the sponsor, not the funds, not the founder, and not any equityholder. Part 8 requires that this be stated expressly in the engagement letter, and that a separate non-representation acknowledgement be delivered to the founder.

**Names screened.** Fourteen names were submitted: the seven entities named in Part 3, plus the six existing and former names disclosed by the Prospective Client (including "TechBridge Solutions LLC" and "TB Workflow Systems"), plus "Marcus D. Ferreira."

**Matching method.** The index applies a two-pass screen. Pass one is an exact match on the normalized name (uppercase, punctuation and diacritics stripped, entity suffix retained). Pass two is a fuzzy match reporting normalized similarity, computed as one minus the edit distance between the two normalized names divided by the length of the longer normalized name, expressed on a 0 to 100 scale. Under Section 6.3 of the New Business Intake Policy, any pass-two result at or above 85.0 is routed to a human analyst and may not be auto-cleared. Results below 85.0 are logged and auto-cleared, and the log is sampled quarterly.

**Search window.** No date limit. Closed matters are included without cutoff because the firm's former-client obligations under Model Rule 1.9 do not expire.

## 7. Conflicts Analysis

### 7.1 Framework

Three questions govern. First, is any person or entity the firm proposes to represent directly adverse to a current client, or is there a significant risk that the representation will be materially limited by a responsibility to a current client, a former client, a third person, or a personal interest of a lawyer, under Model Rule 1.7(a)? Second, does any former representation of the firm bear a substantial relationship to this matter under Model Rule 1.9(a)? Third, is any individual lawyer's disqualification imputed to the firm under Model Rule 1.10(a)?

Corporate affiliation alone does not make an affiliate a client. Under ABA Formal Opinion 95-390 and Model Rule 1.7, cmt. [34], a lawyer who represents a corporation does not thereby represent its parent, its subsidiaries, or its sister companies unless the circumstances create a reasonable expectation to the contrary, the entities operate as a single unit, or the engagement agreement so provides. The analysis below applies that principle rather than treating the whole Meridian Crest structure as one client.

### 7.2 Common-owner analysis: HealthFirst Technologies, Inc. (co-115)

HealthFirst is a current client. Fund II holds 60% of HealthFirst and Fund III holds 30%. Fund III holds 80% of the Prospective Client. The common link is Meridian Crest Capital Management, LLC, which manages both funds and controls the general partner of Fund III.

This is not a direct adversity conflict. The firm is not proposing to act adverse to HealthFirst; HealthFirst is neither a party to the auction nor, as reported, a prospective bidder. The Rule 1.7(a)(1) analysis is therefore negative on the facts as disclosed.

The Rule 1.7(a)(2) question is closer and turns on two facts. First, Evelyn K. Brandt sits on both boards and is the principal decision-maker for the sponsor on both investments. Second, the auction process will generate valuation, customer-concentration, and product-roadmap information about TechBridge that a lawyer holding it could not readily quarantine from advice given to HealthFirst on adjacent healthcare-technology matters. The risk is a material-limitation risk running to information flow and to the firm's incentive to preserve the sponsor relationship, not a risk of adversity between the two portfolio companies today.

Two facts materially change this analysis if they occur, and both are made conditions in Part 8: HealthFirst joining the bidder list, and any HealthFirst-TechBridge commercial negotiation opening during the auction period.

**Conclusion.** Not a present Rule 1.7(a)(1) conflict. A Rule 1.7(a)(2) risk that is consentable and that the firm should manage with an information screen and a written waiver from HealthFirst.

### 7.3 Former-client analysis: TechBridge Solutions Ltd. (matter 23-0774)

The firm's Dublin office represented TechBridge Solutions Ltd. in a closed employment matter, opened February 6, 2023 and closed December 19, 2023. The former client is a member of the Prospective Client's own corporate family, and the proposed representation is not adverse to it. Model Rule 1.9(a) is not triggered, because the firm would not be representing a person whose interests are materially adverse to the former client.

The material point is not a conflict but a records point. Information the firm holds from matter 23-0774 concerning the Irish entity's employment liabilities is confidential information of a former client under Model Rule 1.9(c). Because the buyer will diligence that entity, the deal team must not use or disclose 23-0774 material except to the extent the Prospective Client, as parent and as the successor in interest to the corporate family's information, authorizes in writing. Condition C-4 in Part 8 addresses this.

### 7.4 Personal-interest analysis: Rule 1.7(a)(2) and Rule 1.10

Ms. Oyelaran's limited partner interest in Fund II is passive, is less than 0.1%, is not a controlling interest, and gives her no role in the auction. Under Model Rule 1.7(a)(2) as applied by the firm's Personal Interest Policy Section 3.1, a passive interest below the 1% threshold in a fund that does not hold the transaction subject does not create a material-limitation conflict. No disqualification arises and therefore nothing is imputed under Model Rule 1.10(a). Ms. Oyelaran will nonetheless be walled from the 26-0912 team as a prophylactic measure (Condition C-2), because she is the HealthFirst relationship partner and the same wall serves the Part 7.2 analysis.

### 7.5 Director-relationship analysis: Marcus D. Ferreira and Atticus Dundee Inc. (co-002)

Mr. Ferreira is a director of Atticus Dundee Inc., an existing client, and simultaneously the founder, CEO, and 20% indirect equityholder of the Prospective Client. Three distinct risks follow.

**Risk one, confusion about who the client is.** Mr. Ferreira interacts with the firm in his capacity as a director of co-002 and will now interact with the firm as the chief executive of a different client. Model Rule 1.13(f) requires the firm to make clear, when the organization's interests are adverse to those of the constituent with whom the lawyer is dealing, that the lawyer represents the organization. Here the risk is one step removed but real, and the answer is the same: a written non-representation acknowledgement to Mr. Ferreira personally, which is Condition C-3.

**Risk two, information flow to co-002.** Mr. Ferreira sits on the audit committee of co-002. If the firm learns, in the course of representing co-002, information about co-002's acquisition appetite or competitive posture that is relevant to the TechBridge auction, the firm cannot use it for TechBridge, and cannot in practice unlearn it. Conversely, TechBridge deal information could reach co-002's board through Mr. Ferreira without any act by the firm. The firm's exposure is limited by the screen in Condition C-2 but not eliminated. This is disclosed to both clients under Condition C-1.

**Risk three, co-002 as a potential bidder.** The requesting attorney has represented that co-002 is not a potential bidder. That representation must be confirmed by co-002's general counsel in writing before the bidder list is circulated. If co-002 bids, the firm has a direct Rule 1.7(a)(1) adversity between two current clients in the same transaction, which the firm will not undertake on a consent basis. Condition C-5 addresses this and states the withdrawal consequence in advance.

### 7.6 Issues expressly reserved

This record does not clear any bidder. It does not clear any financing source, and Meridian Crest's lender relationships were not screened because the capital structure of the transaction is not yet known. It does not address whether the firm may act for the sponsor on any rollover or stapled-financing arrangement, which would be a separate engagement and a separate record.
