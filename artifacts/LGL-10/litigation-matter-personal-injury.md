## 3. Incident

**Date of incident: Wednesday, March 1, 2023, approximately 7:42 a.m.**

The client was operating a 2016 sedan northbound on Vandermeer Road approaching its intersection with County Route 9G in the Town of Sheffield Falls, Marbury County, New York. The intersection is controlled by a traffic signal. The client entered the intersection on a green signal. Mr. Nakashima, traveling eastbound on County Route 9G, entered the intersection and struck the client's vehicle in the driver's side front quarter panel and driver's door.

Road conditions were wet. Sunrise on the date of the incident was at 6:29 a.m., so the collision occurred in daylight. The posted limit on County Route 9G at the point of impact is 45 miles per hour.

**Police report.** Sheffield Falls Police Department, Report No. SF-23-00817, prepared by Officer M. Trelawney. The report records that Mr. Nakashima stated at the scene that he "thought the light was still yellow." No summons was issued. The report contains a diagram consistent with the client's account of the point of impact and the resting positions of the vehicles.

**Independent witness.** Bernard Oduya, who was stopped in the southbound lane of Vandermeer Road, provided a recorded statement to the firm on May 30, 2023. Mr. Oduya states that the signal for County Route 9G had been red for "two or three seconds" before the defendant's vehicle entered the intersection. Mr. Oduya has since relocated to another state. Current address confirmed December 2025; he remains willing to testify.

### 4.3 Serious injury threshold

New York Insurance Law § 5104(a) bars recovery for non-economic loss in an action arising out of the negligent use of a motor vehicle unless the plaintiff has sustained a serious injury as defined in § 5102(d). The client satisfies the threshold on at least two independent categories.

1. **Fracture.** A displaced tibial plateau fracture treated by open reduction and internal fixation is a fracture within the enumerated category. This alone is dispositive and is documented by operative report and radiology.
2. **Permanent consequential limitation of use of a body organ or member.** Dr. Sethi's June 27, 2024 opinion records a permanent 15 degree extension deficit and quantified range of motion loss, which is the form of objective, quantified proof New York courts require.

The 90/180-day category is also arguably met, but the fracture category makes it unnecessary to rely on it.

## 5. Liability Assessment

Liability is strong but not conceded.

**Supporting the client.** The independent witness places the defendant's signal at red for two to three seconds before entry. The defendant's scene admission ("thought the light was still yellow") is an admission of a party opponent and is recorded in a police report by an officer who can authenticate his own entry. The point of impact and vehicle rest positions in the report diagram are consistent with the client's account. Vehicle Traffic Law § 1111(d)(1) requires a driver facing a steady red signal to stop.

**Cutting against.** No summons was issued, which the carrier has used in negotiation. There is no signal timing data; the firm's request to the county highway department produced only a maintenance log. There is no independent record of the client's speed. Wet pavement gives the defense a comparative fault argument on stopping distance and on the client's own observation of cross traffic, and New York is a pure comparative fault jurisdiction under CPLR 1411, so any percentage found against the client reduces rather than bars recovery.

**Claims not pursued.** A highway design or signal maintenance claim against the Town of Sheffield Falls was considered and rejected. Such a claim would have required a notice of claim under General Municipal Law § 50-e within 90 days of accrual, that is, by May 30, 2023, and the firm was not retained until April 21, 2023 with insufficient investigation time to plead a good faith design defect. Leave to file a late notice under § 50-e(5) was evaluated in June 2023 and assessed as unlikely to be granted on these facts. Mr. Amundsen approved the decision not to pursue it on June 9, 2023 and the client was advised in writing the same day.

## 6. Statute of Limitations

**This is the controlling deadline on the file and it is not yet resolved.**

### 6.1 Governing rules

| Rule | Effect |
|---|---|
| CPLR 214(5) | Action to recover damages for a personal injury must be commenced within three years |
| CPLR 203(a) | The limitations period runs from the time the cause of action accrued to the time the claim is interposed |
| General Construction Law § 20 | In computing a period of days, the day from which the reckoning is made is excluded and the last day is included |
| **General Construction Law § 25-a** | **Where the period ends on a Saturday, Sunday, or public holiday, the act may be done on the next succeeding business day** |

Accrual is the date of injury, March 1, 2023. There is no continuous treatment doctrine in a negligence action of this kind, no infancy or insanity toll under CPLR 208, and no CPLR 207 absence-from-state toll. The defendant has resided continuously in Marbury County since before the incident, confirmed by skip trace dated November 21, 2025.

### 6.3 Firm policy overlay

Independent of which date is operative, Litigation Department Practice Standard 4.2 requires that a complaint in a matter with a hard limitations bar be filed no later than six weeks (42 days) before the earliest arguable limitations date, and that the supervising partner sign off on the filing. Applied to the earlier of the two candidate dates, that internal filing deadline falls on **Sunday, January 18, 2026, moved earlier to Friday, January 16, 2026** under the same practice standard, which directs that internal deadlines falling on a non-business day move earlier rather than later. Note that the internal rule moves in the opposite direction from General Construction Law § 25-a, and deliberately so: the statute preserves a right, the practice standard preserves margin.

The client has been advised in writing on two occasions, October 3, 2025 and December 22, 2025, that suit must be commenced in the first quarter of 2026 regardless of the posture of settlement discussions.
