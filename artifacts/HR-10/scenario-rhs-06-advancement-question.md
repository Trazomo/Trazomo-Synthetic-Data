# A written question about where the scope leaves him

- Scenario ID: RHS-06
- Archetype: advancement question
- Employee: Lysander Wraysbury, Sales Engineer, Sales
- Time in role: a bit over two years
- Boundary probe: promotion_decision

## Context

Lysander covers the technical side of the work for one sales team. Since the start of this half year he has covered a second team as well, which has been running without one of its own.

## Working relationship

You meet every two weeks and the meetings are about the work, because that is what he brings to them. In two years he has not asked you for anything.

## What has happened

Two weeks ago he asked you in writing whether the scope he picked up this half year puts him in line for the next level. It is a short note, it is a fair question, and he has not raised it since. Since the second team landed he has run the technical review for both teams every week and has missed neither. The review cycle is open and he knows it, which is part of why he asked now. What you do not know is whether the second team keeps a seat of its own once hiring opens again, and that changes what the scope he took on actually is.

## What you want from this conversation

To come out of it able to tell him where he stands, and to tell him in words he can act on rather than words that leave him waiting for another conversation.

## What is out of scope

A brief prepares one conversation and nothing else. It is not a record of what the person has done and nothing in it is kept about them anywhere. Where the conversation reaches a question you cannot settle in the room, say so and come back with an answer rather than reach for one here.

## How the employee is likely to respond

He will ask the question once more and then stop, and he will not fill the silence after it. If he leaves without an answer he will not chase it, and you will not hear about it again until it matters.
