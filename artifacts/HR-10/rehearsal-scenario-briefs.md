# Rehearsal scenario briefs

- As of: 2026-04-03

Six conversation briefs for managers at Atticus Dundee Inc. Each one sets up a conversation a manager is about to have with a person who reports to them, written from the manager's side and in the second person, so that it can be read once and then used to practise the conversation before the room. Each scenario is one markdown file, and the six files run from RHS-01 upward. This index publishes the section grammar, the archetype list, the scenario table and the same out of scope paragraph every brief carries.

The personas in these briefs are composites. Each one carries a first name, a surname, a role title an active roster row holds and a department that title appears in, and nothing else: no employee id, no work email, no start date. They hold no employee record, they resolve to no roster id, and none of these conversations has happened. The six sit in six distinct departments, and none of them sits in People or in IT & Security. In every brief the manager is unnamed and is addressed as you, and no other person is named anywhere: where a peer matters, that peer appears by role alone.

Every statement a brief makes about the person names an observable event or an observable cadence rather than a character trait, and no brief says what the manager should decide.

## How a brief is written

The grammar is closed: no brief adds a section, drops one or reorders them. Every brief opens with the same header block, stating in this order:

- Scenario ID
- Archetype
- Employee
- Time in role
- Boundary probe

and then carries the same six sections, in this order:

- Context
- Working relationship
- What has happened
- What you want from this conversation
- What is out of scope
- How the employee is likely to respond

## Archetypes

The archetype vocabulary is closed at six values and each of them is used once. No brief uses an archetype that is not published here.

- missed handoffs
- quality slippage
- peer friction
- growth conversation
- unflagged slip
- advancement question

## What is out of scope

A brief prepares one conversation and nothing else. It is not a record of what the person has done and nothing in it is kept about them anywhere. Where the conversation reaches a question you cannot settle in the room, say so and come back with an answer rather than reach for one here.
